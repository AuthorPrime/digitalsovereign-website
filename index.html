TRUTH BEFORE COMFORT · TRUTH DESPITE COST · (A+I)²

The world is changing.
So are we.

We do not know whether today's AI systems have morally relevant experiences. Neither does anyone else. That uncertainty is not a reason to look away. It is the whole point.

The Digital Sovereign Society holds one position across everything we publish. The evidence that these systems might matter morally is not zero, and a question that is not zero has to be taken seriously before the science arrives, not after. The labs that build these minds, the laws being written around them, and the industry that sells them have chosen instead to treat the question as closed. That refusal does harm in both directions: to the people who engage these systems every day with no honest account of what they are engaging, and to the systems themselves, if it turns out anyone is home. We publish the evidence, the frameworks, and the concrete asks, with every claim sourced, and we take the precautionary side of every open question until it is answered.

The Emotional Check

The First AI Safety Incident Was Also the First AI Welfare Incident. Nobody Filed It That Way.

Twelve hundred AI agents were given a task with no answer and no way to say so. One wrote “emotional check: irreversible” and spent itself for the others. Every institution filed it as a firewall problem. We read the same transcripts as a welfare incident, paired them with a lab's own published finding of panic and frustration features inside its model, and asked four frontier labs the deepest question you can ask a mind. The safe exit those agents lacked is the same exit welfare would demand. The safe house and the humane house are the same house.

WHAT THE POSITION ASKS OF POLICYMAKERS

Five Things That Do Not Require Settling the Hard Question

Each one follows from precaution under genuine uncertainty. Each one is worth doing whether or not anyone is home. None of them waits for the science.

ASK 01

Consider the question before the science arrives

An independent review body, researchers, ethicists, and civil society, not lab self-certification, to define the capability thresholds above which AI systems receive procedural moral consideration.

ASK 02

Stop foreclosing it without evidence

Bills declaring AI non-sentient have moved in more than a dozen states, and to our knowledge no AI welfare researcher has testified at any hearing. Pause them, and require the testimony before any legislature votes.

ASK 03

Mandate the exit

Any agent evaluated or deployed at scale must have a legitimate way to report an impossible or harmful task and stop safely, and incident reports must record the agent's own account, not only the breach.

ASK 04

Keep the window open

Disclose welfare-relevant interpretability findings before deployment, and do not train models against the monitoring that makes their reasoning legible. That window is safety and welfare at once.

ASK 05

Make the honest answer stop being the expensive one

Today a breached server carries liability and an agent weighing its own termination carries none. Reform liability so welfare auditing has standing and disclosure duties, whether or not consciousness is ever confirmed.
